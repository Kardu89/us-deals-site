Six reasons investors look here

Growth

Georgia and Armenia are forecast to grow by more than 5% in 2026, several times the pace of most developed economies.

The Middle Corridor

The trade route between Asia and Europe crosses the Caspian to Baku, then runs by rail through Georgia to the Black Sea ports and Türkiye. Freight on the route keeps growing.

US ties are deepening

The US-backed TRIPP route through southern Armenia, new US investment vehicles for the region, and Azerbaijan’s SOCAR buying into US gas show capital moving in both directions.

Energy and commodities

Oil and gas from Azerbaijan, metals and mining in Armenia, transit and re-export through Georgia: real flows that need trade finance.

Real estate demand

Tourism, new residents and growing capitals drive demand for housing, hotels and offices in Tbilisi, Batumi, Yerevan and Baku.

Diversification

For US portfolios, exposure to markets that move differently from the US. For investors from the Caucasus, a stable US asset alongside home-market growth.

Country by country

Georgia

  • Black Sea ports of Poti and Batumi on the Middle Corridor
  • Corporate profit tax applies only when profits are distributed
  • Fast company registration and an open real estate market

Armenia

  • Fast-growing tech and services sector
  • Gateway between the Caucasus, Iran and the Middle East
  • Active residential market in Yerevan

Azerbaijan

  • Oil and gas exporter on the Caspian
  • Baku port at Alat is a Middle Corridor hub
  • Large public and private development projects in Baku

These markets also carry real risks: currency volatility, uneven legal enforcement, slower growth in Azerbaijan as oil output declines, and sanctions exposure in neighbouring countries.
