Azerbaijan–Georgia trade up 42% as the Middle Corridor fills

Azerbaijan is Georgia's largest investor with close to USD 4 billion, and the Baku-Tbilisi-Kars railway now runs at full capacity.

The numbers

Trade between Azerbaijan and Georgia grew 42% in the first nine months of 2026, Azerbaijan’s Prime Minister Ali Asadov said at the 11th meeting of the two countries’ Intergovernmental Commission on Economic Cooperation. Nine-month trade has already passed last year’s full-year total and is close to USD 1 billion.

Azerbaijani customs data for January to September show USD 888 million of trade, of which USD 757 million were Azerbaijani exports to Georgia.

Investment and rail

Azerbaijan is Georgia’s largest investor, with investments approaching USD 4 billion, Asadov said. He pointed to the Baku–Tbilisi–Kars railway as an example of the partnership: the line runs at full capacity after its freight capacity was raised to five million tonnes.

Energy is next

In May 2026 the two governments agreed a joint communiqué on gas supplies, electricity trade and transit, and oil transport. Azerbaijan plans to supply electricity to Georgia and to send power through Georgia to Türkiye, and 20-year electricity supply and transit agreements could start from January 2027.

Azerbaijan’s ambassador in Tbilisi has set a goal of more than USD 2 billion in bilateral trade, without a deadline.

What it means for business

  • Growing rail and energy flows between Baku and Tbilisi support logistics, warehousing and industrial real estate along the corridor.
  • Traders moving goods through Azerbaijan and Georgia gain capacity on a route that links Central Asia with Europe.
  • For US investors, the Caucasus leg of the Middle Corridor is already a working trade route.

Keep in mind: Trade figures differ between sources and agencies, and the 20-year energy contracts had not been confirmed as signed at the time of writing.
